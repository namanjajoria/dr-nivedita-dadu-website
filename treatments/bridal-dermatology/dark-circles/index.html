Dark Circles

What Are Dark Circles?

They're the darker or shadowy patches under your eyes that make you look worn out, even on a good day. For some people the colour is actual pigment sitting in the skin. For others, the skin there is so thin that the veins underneath show through as a bluish or purplish tinge. And sometimes there's a small hollow under the eye that simply catches shadow. Plenty of people have two or all three going on. They don't usually fade on their own, and with age they often get a little deeper.

So the first visit is mostly about looking closely. The dermatologist checks how thick your under-eye skin is, how deep any pigment goes, whether you've lost some fat in that area, and how visible the blood vessels are. Once that's clear, your dark circles can be put into a type: pigmented, volume-related, vascular, or mixed. The type is what decides the treatment.

Causes of Dark Circles

  • Ageing skin: the under-eye skin gets thinner and loses support, so veins show through more
  • Losing volume: less fat under the eyes leaves a hollow, and hollows throw shadows
  • Pigment: extra melanin collects in the area, and for many people this simply runs in the family
  • Sun: years without sunscreen darken and weaken skin that's already delicate
  • Sleep and stress: irregular nights and a stressful routine show up first around the eyes
  • Screens: long hours staring at a phone or laptop strain the eyes and can make circles stand out more
  • Not drinking enough water: dehydrated skin looks duller and hollows look deeper
  • City air: pollution keeps the skin mildly irritated, and in Delhi that's hard to avoid

Prevention Tips

  • Put sunscreen under your eyes too: most people stop at the cheeks, so pick a gentle one and take it all the way up
  • Try to sleep at the same time each night: consistency matters more than the odd long lie-in
  • Drink water through the day: well-hydrated skin hides shadows better
  • Give your eyes screen breaks: every so often, look away and rest them for a minute
  • Stop rubbing: it irritates the thin skin and can make pigment worse
  • Keep under-eye skincare simple: strong actives meant for the face can sting and inflame this area, so ask your dermatologist what to use
  • See a doctor if nothing's changing: a quick check tells you whether it's pigment, hollowness or veins, which beats months of trying random creams

FAQs

Will my dark circles go away for good?

Results can last a long while, but most people need the odd top-up. Your skin keeps ageing and life keeps happening, and both show under the eyes.

How do I know which treatment suits me?

You don't have to guess. The dermatologist looks at whether it's pigment, a hollow, visible veins or a combination, and suggests treatment from there.

Isn't it just pigmentation?

Often not. Thin skin showing the veins underneath, or a small hollow casting shadow, is just as common, and those need a different approach.

My skin is sensitive. Is this safe?

Yes, as long as it's done under medical supervision with settings chosen for your skin.

How long before I see a difference?

That depends on the cause and the treatment. Some people notice brighter skin first, others smoother texture or softer hollows. It tends to build slowly.

Can an eye cream fix this?

Usually not on its own. A good cream can support treatment, but for most people it won't solve the problem.

Do screens make dark circles worse?

They can. Long screen hours strain the eyes and make existing circles look more obvious.

Do men get this treated too?

All the time. A lot of our dark circle patients are men.

How much does treatment cost in Delhi?

It varies with the cause, the treatment and how many sessions you need. We'll give you the figure after your consultation.
