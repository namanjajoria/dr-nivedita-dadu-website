Sunscreen Myths vs Facts: What a Dermatologist Actually Wants You to Know

Sunscreen attracts more misinformation than almost any other skincare product, and a lot of it genuinely holds people back from using it consistently. If you've been confused about the right treatment in Delhi's approach to sun protection because of conflicting claims online, here's a clear, myth-by-myth breakdown of what's actually true.

Myth 1: Sunscreen Makes Skin Look Darker

This one has some truth to it, but only under specific conditions. Physical sunscreens, typically containing zinc oxide and titanium dioxide, can cause a noticeable white cast and feel somewhat sticky. This white cast is exactly what can make skin appear darker or ashier once it settles and blends unevenly.

The fix here is straightforward: choose formulations without a heavy white cast, or specifically go for tinted sunscreens. These combine all the benefits of physical sunscreen with iron oxide added in, which camouflages the white cast entirely while giving a foundation-like finish instead.

Concern Solution
White cast, ashy appearance Tinted physical sunscreen with iron oxide
Sticky feeling Newer fluid, lightweight formulations

Myth 2: Sunscreen Causes Vitamin D Deficiency

This is genuinely one of the most persistent myths out there. Yes, Vitamin D is produced through sun exposure, but this deficiency claim assumes people are applying sunscreen to the perfectly correct amount, proper reapplication, covering every sun-exposed area. In reality, most people apply sunscreen sparingly, once or twice a day, often only on the face.

Vitamin D levels also depend heavily on diet and geographic location, not sun exposure alone. Interestingly, farmers who spend entire days outdoors, seen regularly in clinical practice, often still show Vitamin D deficiency despite constant sun exposure. This alone suggests sunscreen isn't the deciding factor people assume it is. If Vitamin D levels are genuinely low, a dedicated supplement is the reliable fix not skipping sunscreen.

Myth 3: "I Applied Sunscreen Properly But Still Got Tanned"

This is a genuinely common complaint, and there are real reasons behind it. Reapplication is often impractical sunscreen washes off in water during beach holidays, and high altitude on hill stations means more direct, intense sunlight. Snow reflects light significantly too, increasing tanning risk further.

That said, sunscreen still matters immensely here. Even with some tanning occurring, sunscreen continues protecting against sunburn and premature ageing benefits that aren't visible day-to-day but matter significantly over time. You're still getting roughly 70% of sunscreen's protective benefit even if some tanning happens despite consistent use.

Sunscreen Formats: Spray, Stick, and Powder

Different formats genuinely serve different purposes, and picking the right one for the right situation matters.

Spray sunscreens work well for large body surface areas where applying cream would be impractical. Since these contain aerosols, they shouldn't be sprayed directly onto the face spray into your hands first, then apply. Direct spraying on the body is fine.

Stick sunscreens are convenient for the eye area specifically, since they won't drip or cause the stinging sensation liquid sunscreen often causes near the eyes. Beyond that specific use, reapplying a stick directly onto skin that's already accumulated pollution, dirt, and makeup throughout the day isn't particularly hygienic.

Powder sunscreens work best as a top-up option rather than primary protection. They typically have lower SPF but offer a matte, foundation-like finish, making them genuinely useful for touch-ups during the day.

Format Best Use
Spray Large body areas (not directly on face)
Stick Eye area touch-ups only
Powder Reapplication/touch-ups, not primary protection

Does SPF in Makeup Count Toward Your Sunscreen?

No, and this is a genuine misconception worth correcting. If your compact has 15 SPF, your moisturizer has 15 SPF, and your foundation has 15 SPF, that doesn't add up to 45 SPF combined. Treat each product's SPF as capped at its own individual number, regardless of how many SPF-containing products you layer. You still need a dedicated sunscreen with at least 40 SPF applied separately, relying on cosmetic products' built-in SPF simply isn't sufficient protection on its own.

Chemical Sunscreen Filters: What's the Concern?

Chemical sunscreens contain filters like avobenzone, oxybenzone, and octyl methoxycinnamate. Oxybenzone specifically has faced scrutiny over potential hormonal disruption. The research isn't entirely conclusive, but given that uncertainty, physical sunscreen is the safer choice, particularly for sensitive skin, pregnancy, lactation, or anyone trying to conceive, since physical filters aren't absorbed the way chemical filters are.

Myth 4: Sunscreen Causes Cancer

This is a genuine, if common, misconception worth addressing directly. Sunscreen-related cancer isn't well established or common, while skipping sunscreen genuinely increases skin cancer risk through UV exposure. Indian skin also carries comparatively lower skin cancer risk overall, but the "sunscreen causes cancer" claim itself is simply not accurate.

Should I Apply Sunscreen Indoors?

Yes, genuinely even working from home requires sunscreen. Glass windows only block UVB rays; UVA rays still penetrate glass and can cause skin damage and premature ageing indoors. Checking your sunscreen's PA rating tells you how well it protects against UVA specifically, so choosing a sunscreen with a solid PA rating matters even for indoor days. For those working on laptops for extended hours, physical sunscreen offers added protection against blue light exposure too.

Situation Sunscreen Still Needed?
Working from home Yes, UVA penetrates glass
Cloudy weather Yes, UV rays still present
Laptop/screen use Yes, physical sunscreen offers blue light protection

Sunscreen for Kids

Children genuinely need sunscreen too, especially with outdoor school activities, sports, or swimming. Physical sunscreen is the better choice here free of chemical filters, genuinely safer, and offering better protection for their more delicate skin.

Getting the Right Sunscreen for Your Skin

Sunscreen selection genuinely depends on skin type and lifestyle, and this is exactly the kind of practical guidance that comes up often during consultations at Dadu Medical Centre. For dry skin, sunscreens with hyaluronic acid work well; for sensitive skin, physical, tinted, or ceramide-based formulations are ideal. For oily skin, lighter formulations with ingredients like watermelon extract suit better. If you're travelling or working from home with heavy screen exposure, tinted physical sunscreens remain the most reliable choice across the board.

For anyone exploring a proper skin clinic in Delhi to find the right sunscreen for their specific skin type and lifestyle, consulting a dermatologist in Delhi helps cut through the noise of trending myths and get a genuinely evidence-based recommendation.

Final Thoughts

Sunscreen myths persist largely because they contain a grain of truth stretched into something misleading. Understanding the actual mechanisms of how white cast happens, why Vitamin D deficiency isn't really about sunscreen, and why indoor UVA exposure still matters makes it much easier to build a consistent sun protection habit without second-guessing every claim you come across online.
