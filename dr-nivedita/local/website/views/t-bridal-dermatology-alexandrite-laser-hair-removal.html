How the Alexandrite Laser Works

The laser fires light through an alexandrite crystal at 755nm. That light passes through the skin and gets absorbed by melanin inside the hair follicle, where it turns into heat. The heat damages the root, and the follicle has a much harder time growing hair again.

There's a catch, and it's the same for every hair removal laser. Only hair in its active growth phase responds. The rest is resting and will come up later, which is why a course of sessions is needed rather than a single visit. The handpiece cools the skin around each pulse, so the surface stays protected while the follicle gets the heat.

Alexandrite or Diode?

Both are used at the clinic, and they suit different people. Alexandrite, at 755nm, is designed with fine and thinner hair in mind, which makes it a strong option for facial hair in women. Diode, at around 800 to 810nm, goes a little deeper and is usually the better pick for thick, coarse, dark hair. Diode also tends to be gentler on darker skin.

Which one you get isn't something you have to work out yourself. Dr. Nivedita Dadu's team looks at your hair, your skin tone and the area before choosing.

As for waxing, threading and shaving, they're quick but temporary, and repeated waxing can leave the skin rough or prone to ingrown hairs. Laser aims for long-term reduction instead.

Areas That Can Be Treated

Facial areas are where Alexandrite gets used most: the upper lip, chin, full face and eyebrows. It's also used for underarms, full arms and legs, and the bikini line. For men, the back, chest, shoulders, neckline and beard shaping are common. You can have more than one area done in the same appointment.

Ideal Candidates

Women with fine facial hair on the upper lip, chin or jawline are the classic fit, and so is anyone with thinner body hair that hasn't responded well to other methods. Men looking to shape eyebrows or tidy the beard line can benefit too. It generally works best on fair to medium skin.

Things to know: Laser needs pigment to work, so very light, grey or blonde hair may not respond much, even to Alexandrite. People with darker skin are often better suited to diode or Nd:YAG settings, which your doctor will decide at the consultation.

FAQs

What is the Alexandrite laser best for?

Fine, thinner hair, especially soft facial hair in women. It works at 755nm, a wavelength that picks up finer hair better than many other lasers.

Does it hurt?

Most people find it mild. The handpiece cools the skin with each pulse, which keeps things comfortable.

How many sessions will I need?

Usually 6 to 8, planned after your hair type and growth cycle are assessed. Brow or beard shaping often needs 4 to 6.

Why isn't one session enough?

Only follicles in their growth phase respond to the laser. Spacing out sessions lets each new batch get treated.

Is Alexandrite safe for Indian skin?

It can be, with the right settings chosen by a trained professional. For darker skin tones, your doctor may suggest diode instead.

Can women use it for facial hair?

Yes, it's one of the main reasons people choose Alexandrite. Upper lip and chin are the most common areas.

How is a clinic laser different from a salon laser?

Clinics use medical-grade machines operated by trained staff under a doctor's supervision. Salon devices are often lower-grade and run by less trained technicians.

Can I get my face and body done in one visit?

Yes. Several areas can be treated in the same appointment.
