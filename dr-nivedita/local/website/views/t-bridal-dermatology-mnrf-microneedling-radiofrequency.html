How MNRF Works

The needles are insulated along their length, so the radiofrequency energy comes out at the tips, below the epidermis. That's where the scar tissue and collagen fibres are. The outer layer of skin gets very little of the heat, which is why the surface heals quickly.

Two things happen at once. The tiny needle punctures switch on the skin's own healing response and release growth factors. The RF pulse then heats the deeper tissue, breaking down the old, tight collagen bands and prompting fibroblasts to lay down new collagen. Put together, that's a stronger push for repair than needling or heat would give on their own.

After your consultation, the dermatologist decides whether MNRF on its own is enough or whether it should be followed by Collagen Induction Therapy, depending on your scars.

MNRF vs Regular Microneedling

People often ask about this. Regular microneedling uses needles alone, which creates small controlled injuries that nudge collagen production. MNRF adds radiofrequency heat through those same needles, so the effect reaches deeper and tends to be stronger, which matters for pitted acne scars.

What MNRF Can Help With

Acne scars are the main reason people book it, particularly the depressed, pitted kind left by cystic or deep inflamed acne. As old scar tissue breaks down and new collagen fills in, those dips gradually look shallower.

Stretch marks from pregnancy, weight changes or growth spurts can also soften in texture and colour. Large pores often look tighter as the collagen around them rebuilds, and skin that's dull or uneven usually looks smoother overall.

Benefits

The biggest difference compared with creams and peels is depth. MNRF reaches the layer where scars actually sit, and it uses heat plus needling to rebuild that layer rather than just polishing the surface. Because the energy goes in below the epidermis, downtime is short, usually a day or two of redness. And since surface pigment isn't heavily targeted, it's considered a good option for darker skin, which tends to be prone to marks after more aggressive treatments.

Ideal Candidates

MNRF is a good fit if you have pitted or uneven acne scars, stretch marks you'd like to soften, or enlarged pores that make skin look rough. It's often recommended for Indian and darker skin tones for the reasons above. The dermatologist will look at how deep and severe the scars are before planning sessions.

Process & Techniques

It starts with a consultation, where the dermatologist assesses your scars or stretch marks and decides on MNRF alone or MNRF followed by Collagen Induction Therapy. On the day, a numbing cream is applied first. Once it's taken effect, the MNRF handpiece is moved over the area. Most people describe a mild warmth or tingling rather than pain.

How many sessions? For acne scars, most people need six to eight sessions, three to four weeks apart. Deeper or more severe scarring can take longer. Texture usually starts to look better after two or three sessions, and the bigger change builds as the course goes on. Results vary from person to person.

Recovery: Expect some redness, a bit like mild sunburn, for one to two days. There's no significant downtime, and most people are back to their normal routine within a day or two.
