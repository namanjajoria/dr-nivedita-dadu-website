PCOS Explained: Causes, Symptoms, and How to Manage It Long-Term

PCOS is one of those conditions that gets talked about constantly, yet a lot of genuine confusion still surrounds what it actually is, whether it's dangerous, and why it seems to affect so many more people than it did a generation ago. If you're researching the right PCOS​ treatment in Delhi for PCOS-related symptoms, whether gynaecological or cosmetic, understanding the condition properly is the first real step toward managing it.

What Is PCOS, Really?

PCOS, or Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome (sometimes called PCOD), is fundamentally a lifestyle issue rather than a disease in the traditional sense. It occurs when multiple immature cysts form in the ovaries, and these cysts produce hormones that trigger a range of symptoms from gynaecological issues like irregular periods and difficulty conceiving, to cosmetic symptoms like unwanted facial hair, weight gain, acne, and hair fall.

What Causes PCOS?

Several factors contribute here. Stress is a genuinely common trigger, alongside a sedentary lifestyle sitting for long stretches without regular exercise. Diet plays a significant role too, particularly high-glycemic-index foods. Genetics matter as well; if your mother had PCOS, your likelihood increases. Smoking is worth flagging specifically, since it raises androgen (male hormone) levels in the body, which can worsen PCOS.

Recognising the Symptoms: Gynaecological vs. Cosmetic

Gynaecological symptoms include having fewer than nine periods a year, scanty or delayed periods, spotting, or difficulty conceiving.

Cosmetic symptoms are often what bring people to a dermatologist rather than a gynaecologist. Acne appearing after age 25, concentrated on the jawline and lower face, nodular and painful, resistant to standard pimple creams this pattern strongly suggests PCOD-related acne rather than ordinary breakouts. Hair thinning is another key sign not necessarily dramatic shedding, but hair gradually becoming lighter, partings widening, hair going flat and lifeless over a few years without an obvious cause. Unwanted facial hair, or hair growth in typically male patterns like the chest or back, can also indicate hormonal imbalance. Acanthosis nigricans pigmentation on the neck, underarms, or thighs caused by insulin resistance is another cosmetic marker worth recognising. Difficulty losing weight, or weight gain that seems disproportionate to diet and activity, rounds out the list.

Understanding Insulin Resistance

Insulin resistance is closely tied to PCOS and worth understanding on its own. Insulin is the hormone responsible for helping the body utilise glucose. When insulin can't do this effectively, blood sugar rises, and the body compensates by producing even more insulin creating a cycle where both insulin and sugar levels climb simultaneously. This can lead to weight gain, pigmentation, and eventually diabetes, and it underlies most pre-diabetic conditions as well.

Can You Self-Diagnose PCOS?

Not entirely, but recognising the symptoms is the first step toward getting properly evaluated. If gynaecological symptoms dominate, a gynaecologist is the right first stop; if cosmetic symptoms are more prominent, a dermatologist is fully equipped to diagnose and manage PCOS from that angle. Diagnosis typically involves blood hormone tests and an ultrasound, though in many cases, the clinical picture alone is clear enough without extensive lab work.

The Rotterdam criteria is commonly used for diagnosis; it requires meeting at least two of three conditions: polycystic ovaries visible on ultrasound, irregular or scanty periods, and abnormal hormone blood tests or clinical symptoms of PCOS.

Managing PCOS: Diet and Lifestyle First

Diet correction is genuinely the foundation here. Avoiding high-glycemic foods like burgers, pizza, and pasta matters, but a lesser-known factor is dairy cow and buffalo milk products contain insulin-like growth factor, which can worsen hormonal imbalance. Switching to soy, almond, coconut, or oat milk, and choosing tofu over paneer and plant protein over whey, genuinely helps. White meat like chicken, fish, and eggs is fine, but red meat is worth avoiding, since it increases inflammation in the body. Anti-inflammatory foods strawberries, blueberries, walnuts, and almonds support this further, and Vitamin D supplementation has shown genuine benefit for PCOS management too.

Exercise: Tailored to Your Insulin Levels

If insulin levels are high, cardio becomes genuinely important. If insulin isn't elevated, PCOS-specific yoga postures that target belly fat and fat around the ovaries and uterus work well instead. Strength training also helps, and some clinics now offer machines that simulate abdominal crunches through electromagnetic stimulation capable of the equivalent of 25,000 crunches in half an hour, addressing visceral fat far more efficiently than manual exercise alone.

When Diet and Exercise Aren't Enough

Myo-inositol is a genuinely useful nutritional supplement worth knowing about it's not a hormone, but mimics Vitamin B, and shows real benefit for many patients. If lifestyle changes alone aren't resolving symptoms, hormonal medication becomes the next step. For irregular periods, contraceptive pills may be prescribed. For persistent cosmetic symptoms, dermatologists may recommend medications like spironolactone, and for elevated insulin levels not responding to exercise, metformin is a common prescription.

Is PCOS Dangerous?

Genuinely no, PCOS isn't life-threatening. That said, difficulty conceiving or struggling with persistent cosmetic symptoms and weight can understandably affect self-confidence, sometimes leading to anxiety or low mood. But as a condition itself, PCOS remains manageable rather than dangerous.

"Lean PCOS": Why Weight Isn't the Whole Story

PCOS is often closely associated with weight, but that's genuinely a misconception. Lean PCOS is a recognised category of women with normal weight who still show PCOS symptoms due to high stress, a sedentary lifestyle, poor eating habits, or genetic predisposition, regardless of body weight.

Why PCOS Has Become So Common

This is genuinely a modern lifestyle phenomenon. PCOS has increased significantly over the past few decades alongside rising obesity, heart issues, insulin resistance, and diabetes, all lifestyle-linked conditions that have grown together. Patients who move from less stressful, more active environments hill stations, smaller towns into hectic corporate life in metro cities like Delhi, complete with night shifts and sedentary work, often develop symptoms they never had before.

Can PCOS Be Cured?

Not entirely, it's a lifestyle condition that needs ongoing management rather than a one-time cure. It's genuinely common to see patients whose PCOS was well-managed in their mid-20s return in their mid-30s, after childbirth, with different stress levels, weight, and sleep patterns, finding their symptoms have returned. This underscores that PCOS management is an ongoing commitment, not a single fix.

Final Thoughts

PCOS is genuinely manageable, not dangerous, but it demands ongoing attention rather than a one-time fix. Diet, exercise, stress management, and, where needed, proper medical treatment together offer the most realistic path to keeping symptoms under control long-term, regardless of whether those symptoms show up as irregular periods or as acne and hair thinning on your skin and scalp.
