Dry vs Oily Skin – How to Identify Your Skin Type and What to Do About It

If you have ever stood in a skincare aisle completely confused about which product is actually meant for you, you are probably not alone. Most people guess their skin type based on how their face feels after washing, or they go by what a salesperson once told them years ago. The truth is, a lot of people are treating the wrong skin type entirely and that is exactly why their skin never seems to improve. Dry and oily skin identification in Delhi is something dermatologists at Dadu Medical Centre deal with every single day, because getting this one thing right changes everything about how your skin responds to treatment and products.

So how do you actually tell the difference?

Oily skin tends to produce excess sebum throughout the day. You wake up with shine, your foundation slides off by afternoon, your pores look bigger especially around the nose and forehead, and you are no stranger to blackheads or frequent breakouts. The skin feels thick sometimes, almost congested. Some people with oily skin think that because they have grease on their face they do not need moisturiser which is one of the most common mistakes that makes oily skin worse over time.

Dry skin feels tight. After washing your face, that tight pulling sensation that makes you want to moisturise immediately is dry skin talking. Flaking around the nose, cheeks, or corners of the mouth, a dull uneven complexion, and fine lines that seem more visible than they should be for your age are all signs. Dry skin does not produce enough oil to maintain its natural barrier, which means it is also more reactive, more sensitive, and quicker to show irritation.

Then there is combination skin, which is honestly what a large percentage of people actually have. Oily through the T-zone forehead, nose, chin but dry or normal on the cheeks. This one trips people up the most because products that fix the oily zone tend to dry out the cheeks further, and vice versa.

There is also something called dehydrated skin which people often confuse with dry skin. Dehydrated skin lacks water, not oil. You can have oily skin that is dehydrated at the same time shiny on the surface but tight and dull underneath. This is extremely common in people who over-cleanse, use too many active ingredients, or live in air-conditioned environments for most of the day.

A simple test you can do at home is the blotting paper test. Wash your face with a gentle cleanser, do not apply anything, wait about an hour, then press blotting paper on different zones of your face. If the paper picks up oil all over, you likely have oily skin. If there is barely anything on the paper and your skin feels tight, it is dry. Oil only in the T-zone points toward combination.

But home tests only go so far. If your skin is behaving unpredictably breaking out in some areas, flaking in others, reacting to products it used to tolerate it is worth getting a proper assessment done. A consultation at a good skin clinic in Delhi will use tools like a skin analyser that measures hydration levels, sebum production, pore size, and skin texture at a much more accurate level than any mirror or blotting paper can offer.

At Dadu Medical Centre, Dr. Nivedita Dadu and her team go beyond just labelling your skin type. The goal is to understand why your skin is behaving the way it is. Hormonal changes, diet, climate, stress, the products you are currently using all of these feed into your skin's behaviour and need to be considered before recommending any treatment or skincare routine.

For oily skin, treatments that work well include salicylic acid-based chemical peels that go into the pore and clear out congestion, sebum-regulating facials, and in cases of persistent oiliness, certain laser treatments or carbon facials that reduce sebaceous gland activity over time. For dry skin, hydrating treatments like hyaluronic acid boosters, moisturising facials, and barrier repair protocols make a significant difference. Microneedling with growth factors helps dry and ageing skin rebuild its ability to retain moisture from within.

Combination and dehydrated skin types often benefit the most from a structured treatment plan because there are multiple things to address at once. Doctors in Delhi at Dadu Medical Centre frequently see patients who have been managing combination skin with the wrong products for years and a few targeted sessions combined with the right routine at home can genuinely transform how the skin looks and feels day to day.

Getting your skin type right is not just about vanity. It is about making sure that what you put on your face is actually helping and not quietly making things worse in the background.
