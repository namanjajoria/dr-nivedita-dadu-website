Skin Boosters Guide: Hyaluronic Acid, NAD+, Exosomes, and Collagen Boosters Compared

Most people hear "skin booster" and immediately think of hyaluronic acid hydration shots. Fair enough that's usually where the conversation starts. But if you're looking into Skin Boosters treatment in Delhi options for skin quality, texture, or overall glow, it's worth knowing that skin boosters cover a much wider range than just hydration. There are formulations built around Vitamin C, glutathione, NAD+, collagen, PDRN, polynucleotides, and exosomes, each targeting something slightly different, and often combined into a single syringe so one procedure delivers several benefits at once.

Starting With the Familiar: Vitamin C and Glutathione

Vitamin C boosters do more than just brighten skin; they also support collagen production while working on pigmentation. It's a two-in-one benefit that makes this one of the more versatile options in this category.

Glutathione is something most people have already heard of, usually in the context of oral supplements or topical creams. But glutathione boosters, delivered as an injectable, tend to outperform what you'd get from eating or applying it. The benefits show up more directly in pigmentation control and in reducing oxidative stress at the skin level.

Skin Booster Primary Benefit
Vitamin C Booster Brightening, collagen support, reduces pigmentation
Glutathione Booster Pigmentation control, reduces oxidative stress

The Regenerative Medicine Shift: PDRN and Polynucleotides

Here's where things get more interesting. PDRN and polynucleotides are two distinct formulations, both working within what's now called regenerative medicine, a space that's getting a lot of attention lately, and for good reason. The whole idea behind regenerative treatments is moving away from correction and toward prevention. Rather than needing aggressive anti-ageing procedures later, the goal becomes keeping skin texture, tone, and quality strong enough that you never really need them in the first place. This is sometimes referred to as "pro-ageing" rather than anti-ageing, and it's becoming a fairly popular way of thinking about long-term skin health.

PDRN and polynucleotides are essentially DNA fragments that work specifically on tissue repair. They don't add volume or mask anything; they support the skin's own repair mechanisms, which is a fundamentally different approach compared to fillers or more corrective treatments.

NAD+ Boosters: Why This One's Been Trending

NAD+, or Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide, has been getting a lot of attention recently, and IV drips containing it are already fairly well known. What's less talked about is that NAD+ levels naturally decline as we age, and this decline affects how well the skin manages oxidative stress and stays healthy overall.

Delivered as an injectable rather than through an IV drip or oral route, NAD+ tends to show faster, more noticeable results. And here's something worth knowing whether or not you smoke, NAD+ boosters still offer real benefits, since the decline in NAD+ levels happens with age regardless of lifestyle factors.

Booster Type What It Targets
PDRN / Polynucleotides Tissue repair, regenerative support
NAD+ Booster Oxidative stress control, cellular health
Exosomes Signals fibroblasts to boost collagen production

Exosomes: The Signalling Molecules

Exosomes work a bit differently from the boosters mentioned so far. Rather than directly supplying a nutrient or molecule, they act as signalling molecules essentially sending a message to skin cells that tells fibroblasts to ramp up collagen production. The result is skin that regenerates, repairs, and stays healthier over time, driven by your own cellular activity rather than an external add-on.

All of these Vitamin C, glutathione, NAD+, PDRN, exosomes can be infused into the skin through injectables. And if needles aren't your thing, most of these can also be delivered through a derma pen instead, which makes the whole process more approachable for people who'd rather avoid injections altogether.

Collagen Boosters: Type I and Type III

Beyond these formulations, collagen boosters specifically containing Type I and Type III collagen are also available. When infused into the face, either through injectables or a derma pen, these tend to produce faster and more noticeable results compared to relying on oral collagen supplements alone, largely because the collagen is going directly where it's needed instead of being processed through digestion first.

Delivery Method Best For
Injectable Faster, more concentrated results
Derma pen Needle-free alternative, broader coverage

It's Not Just About Skin

Worth mentioning hair boosters exist too, addressing multiple hair concerns using similar regenerative principles. If hair thinning or scalp health is more of a priority for you right now, that's a separate but related conversation worth having during a consultation.

Choosing the Right Booster for Your Skin

With this many options, picking the right one can genuinely feel overwhelming, and that's exactly where a proper consultation makes a difference rather than guessing based on trending terms. This is a conversation that comes up frequently at Dadu Medical Centre. Dr. Nivedita Dadu often explains that the right combination of boosters really depends on what your skin specifically needs: pigmentation control might call for Vitamin C and glutathione, while someone focused on long-term texture and tissue quality might benefit more from PDRN or polynucleotides. Combining molecules within a single syringe is common precisely because most people have more than one concern at once.

Final Thoughts

Skin boosters have moved well beyond just hyaluronic acid hydration shots. Between Vitamin C, glutathione, NAD+, PDRN, polynucleotides, exosomes, and collagen boosters, there's now a fairly specific formulation for almost every skin concern: pigmentation, hydration, tissue repair, or overall regeneration. Understanding what each one actually does makes it far easier to have an informed conversation with your dermatologist instead of just asking for "the trending one." The right combination really comes down to what your skin needs right now, and that's best figured out with a proper assessment.
