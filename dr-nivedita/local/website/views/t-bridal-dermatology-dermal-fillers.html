It usually starts small. The lines from your nose to your mouth look a bit deeper in photos. Your cheeks seem flatter than they used to be, or the area under your eyes looks sunken by evening. None of it is dramatic, but together it can make you look more tired than you feel.

A lot of that comes down to volume. As we age, skin holds less hyaluronic acid, collagen and elastin, and the soft padding in the face thins out. How Hyaluronic Acid Fillers Work

Hyaluronic acid, or HA, is a kind of sugar your body already makes. Its job is to hold on to water, which keeps skin plump and supple. The filler is a smooth gel made of HA. Once it's injected into a specific layer of the skin, it fills the space where volume has been lost, and because HA draws water towards it, the area also looks a little more hydrated.

The result shows up right away. Over the following months your body slowly breaks the gel down, which is why fillers last a long time but aren't permanent. And if something ever needs adjusting, HA filler can be dissolved with an enzyme.

Areas That Can Be Treated

The most common requests are the smile lines running from nose to mouth (nasolabial folds), cheeks and cheekbones that have flattened, and lips that have thinned or need a bit of shape. Fillers are also used for under-eye hollows, the chin, the jawline and the marionette lines that run down from the corners of the mouth. Fine lines around the mouth and on the forehead can be softened, and the backs of the hands can get some volume back too.

A quick note on under-eyes: fillers help with the hollow that casts a shadow. If your dark circles are mostly pigment, a different treatment will work better, and your doctor will tell you which you have.

Benefits

The biggest plus is how quickly you see a difference. You walk out with the change already visible, and it looks soft rather than stiff when placed properly. Because the gel is made from a substance your body already knows, and can be dissolved if needed, it's one of the more reversible cosmetic options. Results usually last 9 to 18 months, the appointment fits into an hour, and most people go about their day straight afterwards. Fillers can also be combined with anti-wrinkle injections, thread lifts or skin boosters for a fuller refresh.

Ideal Candidates

People in their 30s often come in for early volume loss in the cheeks, lips or smile lines. In the 40s and 50s, it's more common to treat a few areas together, as planned by the doctor. Anyone wanting fuller lips, a sharper chin or jawline, or less hollowness under the eyes can be a good fit. For brides, it's best to have fillers done two to four weeks before the wedding so any swelling has fully settled.

Process & Techniques

The whole appointment, consultation included, takes about 30 to 60 minutes.

It starts with a proper conversation. The doctor talks through what you'd like to change, studies your facial structure, and plans exactly where the filler should go and how much is needed. A numbing cream is then applied. Once it's working, the filler is injected into the planned points. Most people feel some pressure or a quick pinch, not much else.

How long does it last? Usually 9 to 18 months. It depends on the area treated, the type of filler and your own metabolism. A touch-up helps keep the look going.

Recovery: You might have a little swelling, redness or bruising where the needle went in. That usually settles in one to three days, and most people get on with their day straight away.

FAQs

What are dermal fillers made of?

The ones used here are hyaluronic acid gels. HA is a substance your body already produces to keep skin hydrated.

Does getting filler hurt?

Numbing cream goes on first, so most people only feel some pressure or a quick pinch.

How long will the results last?

Usually somewhere between 9 and 18 months, depending on the area and your metabolism. Touch-ups keep things looking fresh.

Are fillers safe?

HA fillers are widely used and have a good safety record when done by a trained doctor. They can also be dissolved with an enzyme if needed.

Can fillers fix dark circles?

They can fill the hollow that causes a shadow under the eyes. They won't help dark circles caused by pigment.

Is there any downtime?

Very little. Some swelling, redness or bruising may show for one to three days.

Can fillers be combined with other treatments?

Yes. They're often paired with anti-wrinkle injections, thread lifts or skin boosters.
