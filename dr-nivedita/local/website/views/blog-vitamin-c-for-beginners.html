Vitamin C for Beginners: How to Start This Powerful Skincare Ingredient the Right Way

17 Aug 2026By Dr. Nivedita Dadu

Vitamin C has genuinely earned its popularity in skincare, and unlike a lot of trending ingredients, this one actually deserves the attention. Why Vitamin C Genuinely Matters for Your Skin

Vitamin C is an antioxidant, and that single classification explains most of its value. Antioxidants protect skin from external damage, free radical exposure, environmental stress, pollution, sun exposure, and UV radiation all fall into this category. Vitamin C works specifically to shield skin from this kind of cumulative external damage.

Beyond that protective role, Vitamin C genuinely helps reduce pigmentation, making it effective for dark spots, uneven skin tone, tanning, and general dullness. It's also hydrating and soothing in its own right, while simultaneously helping reduce fine lines and wrinkles, supporting collagen remodelling, and generally helping skin look more hydrated, rejuvenated, and replenished.

Vitamin C Benefit What It Addresses
Antioxidant protection Free radicals, pollution, UV damage
Pigmentation reduction Dark spots, uneven tone, tanning, dullness
Hydration & soothing Overall skin comfort and texture
Collagen support Fine lines, wrinkles, rejuvenation

Starting Vitamin C as a Beginner

If you're new to Vitamin C, starting at a lower concentration around 10% is genuinely the right approach, and this is best applied at night initially, followed by moisturizer.

Once you're comfortable and have an established skincare routine in place, Vitamin C can shift to daytime use instead. In that case, the order matters: cleanse, apply Vitamin C, follow with moisturizer, then sunscreen. At night, once Vitamin C has moved to your morning routine, you can introduce a stronger active instead of glycolic acid, kojic acid, or retinol for anti-ageing purposes.

Routine Stage Product Order
Beginner (night-only Vitamin C) Cleanse → Vitamin C → Moisturizer
Established routine (Vitamin C moved to day) Cleanse → Vitamin C → Moisturizer → Sunscreen
Night (once Vitamin C shifts to day) Glycolic acid / Kojic acid / Retinol

Choosing the Right Concentration for Your Skin

If you're a genuine beginner, have sensitive skin, or are a teenager just starting with actives, 10% Vitamin C is the appropriate starting point. This concentration is gentle enough that it can even function as the sole active in your routine if your skin is particularly sensitive — meaning a simple structure of moisturizer and sunscreen during the day, with 10% Vitamin C applied at night, is genuinely sufficient on its own.

How to Apply Vitamin C Correctly

The application technique matters more than people realise. After cleansing your face, apply three drops of Vitamin C serum across your entire face, specifically avoiding the under-eye area, since this zone is more delicate and prone to irritation. Follow this with your nighttime moisturizer on top.

Application Step Detail
Cleanse First step, always
Apply Vitamin C 3 drops, full face, avoid under-eye area
Moisturize Apply on top, seals in the serum

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What concentration of Vitamin C should beginners start with?
Ans. Beginners should start with 10% Vitamin C, applied at night initially. This concentration is gentle enough for sensitive skin and teenagers too.

2. Can Vitamin C be used during the day instead of at night?
Ans. Yes, once you have an established routine, Vitamin C can move to daytime use. Apply it before moisturizer and sunscreen for best results.

3. Should Vitamin C be applied near the eyes?
Ans. No, it's best to avoid the delicate under-eye area when applying Vitamin C. Apply it across the rest of the face instead.

4. Why is ferulic acid sometimes added to Vitamin C serums?
Ans. Ferulic acid adds extra antioxidant benefits and helps stabilise the Vitamin C formula. This makes the serum more effective and longer-lasting.

5. Can Vitamin C be the only active in a sensitive skin routine?
Ans. Yes, for sensitive skin or beginners, 10% Vitamin C alone at night can be sufficient. Pair it with a moisturizer and daytime sunscreen.

6. What can I use at night once Vitamin C moves to my morning routine?
Ans. Stronger actives like glycolic acid, kojic acid, or retinol work well at night. These target anti-ageing and pigmentation concerns more intensively.

7. Does Vitamin C help with pigmentation and dullness? Yes, Vitamin C is genuinely effective for dark spots, uneven tone, tanning, and dullness. It works through its antioxidant and brightening properties.
