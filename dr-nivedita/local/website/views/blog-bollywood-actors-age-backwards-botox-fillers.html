The Science of Why Faces Age — And What Stops It

Before getting into the actors it is worth understanding what actually makes a face look older because understanding the mechanism makes the treatments make sense and makes the celebrity results much easier to read.

Volume Loss As we age the fat pads under the skin gradually shrink and shift downward. This is what causes the hollowing under the eyes, the flattening of the cheeks, the deepening of the nasolabial folds, and the general deflation of a face that was once full and rounded. Fillers directly address this by restoring the volume that has been lost.

Bone Resorption The bones of the face actually shrink with age the jaw, the cheekbones, and the orbital bone around the eyes all gradually reduce in volume. This causes the skin to lose its structural support and leads to sagging and repositioning of soft tissue. Strategic filler placement can compensate for some of this structural loss.

Skin Laxity Collagen and elastin production slows with age causing the skin to lose its firmness and elasticity. The result is skin that does not snap back the way it once did and that begins to sag along the jawline and neck.

Dynamic Lines Becoming Static Repeated muscle movement over years gradually etches expression lines into the skin permanently even when the face is at rest. Botox prevents this by relaxing the muscles responsible for these movements before the lines become permanently established.

Skin Surface Changes Sun damage, pigmentation, uneven texture, and dullness accumulate over years of exposure and environmental damage. Skin treatments including laser, chemical peels, and topical medical-grade skincare address these surface changes.

Understanding these five mechanisms explains almost exactly what Botox and fillers do and why they produce such dramatic results when administered correctly. They do not fight ageing in a general sense. They target each specific mechanism of visible ageing with a precise and appropriate treatment.

The Genes Argument What Genetics Can and Cannot Explain

This is the explanation that Bollywood actors reach for most reliably and it deserves a fair assessment rather than immediate dismissal.

Good genetics absolutely contribute to how a face ages. Thicker skin ages more slowly than thin skin. Higher cheekbones maintain their definition longer than flat facial structure. Darker skin tones age more slowly than lighter ones because melanin provides natural UV protection. These things are real.

But genetics has limits. It cannot prevent dynamic lines from forming those are caused by muscle movement and only Botox can interrupt that process. It cannot restore volume that has been lost to the fat pad shrinkage that is a universal consequence of ageing only fillers can do that. It cannot explain why a 60-year-old man has less forehead movement than a 30-year-old. It cannot explain why facial volume increases or stays completely stable between the ages of 40 and 60 when the natural trajectory is consistent loss.

When the specific characteristics of a face align precisely with the specific outcomes of Botox and filler treatment rather than with what genetics and lifestyle produce the more honest and more accurate explanation is treatment. Genes deal the cards. Aesthetic medicine plays them.
