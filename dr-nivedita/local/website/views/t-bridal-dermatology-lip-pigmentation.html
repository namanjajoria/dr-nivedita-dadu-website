Why Lips Turn Dark

Lips get their colour from melanin, the same pigment that gives skin its tone. When the lips start making more of it, or it builds up over time, they look darker or patchy. A few common reasons:

  • Genetics: for some people dark lips run in the family and show up from childhood or the teenage years
  • Smoking: one of the most common causes we see
  • Lots of tea or coffee: regular, heavy intake over the years
  • Lipstick overuse: especially cheaper products with harsher chemicals
  • Sun exposure: lips burn and tan too, and most people never put SPF on them
  • Medicines and hormones: some drug reactions and hormonal changes can darken the lips
  • Dehydration and poor lip care: dry, cracked lips don't help either

Knowing the cause matters. If it's something ongoing, like smoking, the darkness can come back after treatment unless the habit changes too.

How the Laser Works

The clinic uses a nanosecond laser, which fires very short pulses of light. Melanin in the lips absorbs that light, and the pigment breaks into tiny fragments. Over the following days and weeks, your body clears those fragments away naturally.

The laser is aimed at the pigment rather than the surrounding lip tissue, and colour lightens a bit more with every session. Compared with creams and home remedies, which only work on the surface, or lip peels, which can cause a few days of peeling, the laser tends to work better for pigmentation that's been there a long time.

Process & Techniques

  • Consultation: Dr. Nivedita Dadu or a member of her team checks your lips, talks through what's likely causing the darkness and plans the number of sessions.
  • Laser session: The nanosecond laser is passed over the lips. No numbing is used, and most people feel very little during the session.
  • Back to your day: There's no recovery period, and you can apply your usual lip products right away.

How many sessions? More than one, spaced at regular intervals. The exact number depends on how dark the lips are, what caused it, how long it's been there and your skin type. For smokers, sessions are usually 3 to 4 weeks apart. Brides and grooms should ideally start 3 to 4 months before the wedding.

Aftercare and keeping results: Use a lip balm with SPF every day, especially if you're out in the sun a lot. Cutting back on smoking and very frequent tea or coffee helps the results last. Lips can darken again if the original cause continues, so occasional maintenance may be advised.

What to expect: Lightening happens gradually, and most people see a clear difference by the end of their course. Results vary from person to person and can't be guaranteed.

FAQs

Why do lips get darker?

Usually because of extra melanin. Genetics, smoking, a lot of tea or coffee, harsh lipsticks, sun exposure and hormonal changes are the common triggers.

Which laser do you use for dark lips?

A nanosecond laser. It breaks down the excess pigment in the lips, which the body then clears over the following weeks.

Does it hurt?

Most people feel very little. No anaesthesia or numbing cream is used.

Is there any downtime?

No. You can go about your day and apply lip products right after the session.

How many sessions will I need?

That depends on how dark your lips are and what's causing it. Your dermatologist will give you a plan after the consultation.

Will my lips go back to their natural colour?

Most people see significant lightening by the end of the course. How close you get to your original shade varies from person to person.

When should I start before my wedding?

Around 3 to 4 months ahead, so there's enough time for all the sessions.

Can the darkness come back?

It can, if the cause continues, like smoking or skipping sun protection. Daily lip SPF and occasional maintenance help.
