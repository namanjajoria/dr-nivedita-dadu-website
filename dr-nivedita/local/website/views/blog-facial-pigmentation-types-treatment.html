Types of Facial Pigmentation: Melasma, Freckles, PIH, and the Right Treatment for Each

Not all pigmentation is the same, even though it often gets treated that way. Someone with melasma and someone with post-acne marks are usually reaching for the exact same skin-lightening cream, and wondering why one person sees results and the other doesn't. If you've been dealing with dark spots and looking for the right Facial Pigmentation treatment in Delhi, the real answer starts with figuring out exactly which type of pigmentation you're dealing with, because each one responds to something different.

Melasma: The Hormonal One

Melasma shows up far more often in women than men, though men aren't entirely spared. The reason comes down to hormones pregnancy-related melasma, sometimes called the "mask of pregnancy," is a classic example. Beyond pregnancy, heavy sun exposure, thyroid conditions, contraceptive pill use, and the hormonal shifts that come with turning 40 can all trigger or worsen melasma.

Treatment starts with something people often resist: physical or mineral sunscreen. It offers better, longer-lasting sun protection compared to chemical sunscreen, and yes, it needs to go on even indoors. The usual complaint with mineral sunscreens is that they feel greasy or leave a white cast, which is where tinted mineral sunscreen comes in genuinely lightweight, fluid, non-sticky formulas that suit Indian skin tones well, like Uriage's Eclipse Solar Tinted Sunscreen.

On top of sunscreen, tranexamic acid-based creams work particularly well for melasma specifically, with Melano TX Ultra being a reliable, reasonably priced option. Mixed with your night moisturizer, this becomes your evening step, paired with physical sunscreen the following morning.

Supplementation matters here too. Glutathione alone isn't usually enough for melasma; a combination of glutathione with tranexamic acid tends to work better. But this combination counts as medication, not a casual supplement, and needs to be taken only under the guidance of a qualified dermatologist due to potential side effects. If you're just looking for a standalone glutathione supplement without melasma specifically, GSH Forte, which combines glutathione with astaxanthin, is a solid, cost-effective pick.

Freckles: The Genetic One

Freckles show up as brown dots, typically in people who are genetically predisposed meaning it runs in the family or in those living at higher altitudes with more direct sun exposure. Physical sunscreen is essential here too, for the same reasons as melasma, since sun exposure is what drives freckles to darken and spread.

Here's something worth knowing upfront freckles genuinely don't respond well to creams alone. Q-switch laser is typically what's needed for a real, lasting improvement. That said, if you'd still like to try a topical option alongside professional treatment, Aduno's Adobrite Serum is worth considering lightweight, non-sticky, and suitable for use across the full face, not just on freckled areas. Pairing it with a glutathione-astaxanthin supplement like GSH Forte tends to improve results further.

Pigmented Contact Dermatitis

This one commonly shows up on the forehead, and hair dye is a frequent culprit. Reducing hair colour use, or at minimum switching to gentler, lower-chemical formulas, helps significantly. Indigo-based hair dyes specifically are worth avoiding altogether, since they're strongly linked to this kind of pigmentation. Even with regular hair colour, tilting your head back during a wash so dye doesn't trickle onto your forehead makes a real difference.

Beyond hair dye habits, physical sunscreen remains essential, since any pigmentation whether from sunlight, indoor lighting, or even laptop screens tends to darken further with exposure. Minimalist's 2% Arbutin serum works well here, particularly for areas prone to inflammation, helping lighten pigmentation gradually. Worth noting helmet use is another common, lesser-known trigger for this exact pattern on the forehead.

Trigger Prevention Tip
Hair dye (especially indigo-based) Switch to low-chemical formulas, avoid indigo dyes
Helmet use Reduces friction/contact pigmentation over time
Any topical irritant Physical sunscreen limits further darkening

Lichen Planus Pigmentosus: The One That Needs Immediate Attention

This has become increasingly common, particularly among those with thyroid issues or heavy indigo-based hair dye use. It presents differently from the pigmentation types discussed so far. Instead of brown, it shows up as a purple-grey discoloration across the face, indicating pigment deposited much deeper in the skin rather than on the surface.

This is important enough to repeat clearly this type will not respond to any skin-lightening cream. If the pigmentation appears to be spreading, from the face toward the neck or arms, or involving new areas, that's a sign the underlying condition is active. This isn't something to self-treat with over-the-counter creams. It genuinely requires an immediate consultation with an experienced, qualified dermatologist, since managing this condition often involves oral medications to control progression, not just topical treatment.

Pigmentary Demarcation Lines (PDL)

This shows up as symmetrical pigmentation on both sides of the eyes, typically appearing suddenly after age 25 to 30, and it's more common in women. PDL is treated as its own distinct category. Skin-lightening creams can improve it to some extent, alongside physical or mineral sunscreen, and Kojibit Ultra with kojic acid, glycolic acid, and licorice is a solid option worth trying.

One honest thing worth knowing here is that PDL rarely disappears completely. It lightens with treatment but usually stays a shade darker than your surrounding skin tone. In-clinic treatments can help push that improvement further, but a perfect colour match is genuinely difficult to achieve. It's also worth knowing that symmetrical pigmentation around the corners of the lips is another form of PDL, distinct from the classic under-eye version but working on the same underlying principle.

Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)

This is the most commonly seen pigmentation issue, and it happens whenever skin experiences even mild inflammation. Once that inflammation resolves, it settles as brown marks the most familiar version being the marks left behind after acne. The pimple's gone, but the mark lingers.

For PIH, physical or mineral sunscreen remains non-negotiable, and Foxy Glow's Depigmenting Serum is worth recommending here. It contains kojic acid and arbutin, but what sets it apart from most similar creams is the addition of azelaic acid, making it genuinely effective for both red and brown acne marks specifically. Apply it at night, and follow with physical or mineral sunscreen during the day.

Getting the Right Diagnosis Matters More Than the Right Cream

This is exactly the kind of assessment that comes up constantly in consultations at Dadu Medical Centre. Dr. Nivedita Dadu often points out that pigmentation treatment fails most often not because the cream was wrong, but because the diagnosis was wrong to begin with melasma treated like PIH, or lichen planus pigmentosus mistaken for ordinary dark spots, simply won't respond the way you'd expect. If your pigmentation hasn't improved despite trying multiple products, consulting a dermatologist in Delhi is genuinely the fastest way to get an accurate diagnosis rather than continuing to guess.

For anyone researching a proper skin clinic in Delhi that can distinguish between these pigmentation types accurately, this kind of assessment saves significant time and money compared to trial-and-error with skincare aisles. A reliable clinic in Delhi will evaluate your pigmentation's colour, pattern, and progression before recommending a specific treatment path, since each type genuinely needs a different approach.

Final Thoughts

Pigmentation isn't one problem with one solution: melasma, freckles, PIH, PDL, and lichen planus pigmentosus all look similar at a glance but need entirely different treatment strategies. Physical sunscreen is close to universal advice across all of them, but beyond that, getting the right diagnosis is what actually determines whether your treatment works or just wastes months of effort.
