Dermaroller Treatment for Acne Scars

4.8(1,067 Reviews)

Not every acne scar is a deep pit. A lot of people are left with softer, shallower marks: a few rolling dips on the cheeks, some uneven texture that catches the light, the odd small boxcar scar. They're mild, but they still bother you every time you look closely in the mirror. A dermaroller treatment for acne scars in Delhi is often a good starting point for exactly this kind of scarring.

The dermaroller is a small handheld roller covered in very fine medical needles. Rolled carefully over numbed skin, it makes thousands of tiny channels that prompt the skin to repair itself with fresh collagen. While those channels are open, a serum chosen for your concern goes on, so it gets further into the skin than it normally would.

How the Dermaroller Works

The roller's needles are between 0.5 and 1.5mm long, depending on what's being treated. As it's rolled across the skin in overlapping horizontal, vertical and diagonal strokes, the needles make tiny punctures down into the upper and middle layers.

Your skin treats those punctures as small injuries and starts repairing them. Growth factors and cytokines get released, and they set off a healing process that builds new collagen, elastin and fine blood vessels. A bit of pinpoint bleeding during the session is normal, and it's actually part of that growth-factor response. Over the following weeks, the new collagen lifts shallow scars and smooths out rough texture.

At Dadu Medical Centre, every roller is sterile and used once only, and the procedure is done under clinical supervision.

The tiny channels close quite quickly, so serums are applied while they're still open. That's when they can soak in deepest. Which serum you get depends on what's being treated:

Which Scars Respond Best

Dermaroller does best with mild to moderate scarring: shallow rolling scars, early boxcar scars and general surface roughness. If your scars are deeper or more complicated, the doctor may combine dermaroller with Erbium Fibre Laser or MNRF for a stronger effect.

Benefits

It's a fairly gentle way to start rebuilding collagen under acne scars, and the recovery is short, usually a day or two of redness. The serum step means you're getting two things in one session: the needling itself, and better absorption of the products chosen for your skin. It's also flexible. The same treatment can be adjusted for anti-ageing, glow, stretch marks or even the scalp, and it pairs easily with lasers or MNRF when more is needed.

Ideal Candidates

People with mild to moderate acne scars and uneven texture tend to get the most out of it. It also suits those who want collagen support without the downtime of stronger treatments. If your scars are deep, the dermatologist may still use it, just as part of a combined plan.

Process & Techniques

A numbing gel goes on first and stays for about 30 to 45 minutes, so the skin is properly numb before anything starts. The doctor then rolls the dermaroller over the area in overlapping strokes in several directions. Most people feel some pressure or warmth, not much more. The serum for your concern is applied straight after, and the session ends with a soothing silk mask.

How many sessions? Usually four to six, three to four weeks apart. Milder scars may need fewer. Once you're done, a maintenance session every three to six months may be suggested.

What progress looks like: After the first session, redness settles within a day or two and the change is subtle. By the second or third, texture usually feels smoother and scars look a little shallower. Around sessions four and five, the difference in depth and evenness is easier to see. The best texture tends to show four to six weeks after your last session, once the new collagen has matured. Results vary with scar type, skin condition and aftercare.

Recovery: Skin looks flushed or lightly red, a bit like mild sunburn, for 24 to 48 hours. There's no real peeling or open wounds, and most people are back to normal within a day.

Aftercare: Use a gentle, non-comedogenic moisturiser and a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or above every day. Avoid scrubs and strong exfoliating products for a few days, and resist picking or scratching the area. You'll get specific post-care advice after each session.

It's a roller with fine needles that makes tiny channels in the skin. The skin repairs them with fresh collagen, which slowly smooths out mild scars.

Not much. A numbing cream goes on 30 to 45 minutes beforehand, so most people just feel pressure or warmth.

Usually four to six, spaced three to four weeks apart. Milder scars can need fewer.
