Open Pores Treatment Guide: Products and Procedures for Oily, Normal, and Dry Skin

Open pores show up on nearly everyone's skin concern list at some point, yet most people end up using the wrong product for their specific skin type and wonder why nothing's working. If you've been researching Open Pores treatment in Delhi for open pores without much success, understanding what's actually causing them and how that differs between oily and dry skin makes a real difference before you spend on another product.

What Open Pores Actually Are

Open pores are simply the openings of sebaceous glands, the oil-producing glands present in everyone's skin, each attached to a hair follicle. That visible opening is what we call an open pore.

Here's something that surprises a lot of people: open pores show up in both oily skin and dry, mature skin, but for completely opposite reasons. In oily skin, sebaceous glands produce excess oil, which keeps that opening perpetually stretched and visible. In dry, mature skin, it's collagen breakdown and elastin degradation causing the problem as skin loses elasticity and becomes looser, pores appear more prominent simply because the surrounding skin isn't holding them tight anymore.

This distinction matters enormously, because a product that works for oily-skin open pores will do nothing for dry, mature skin dealing with the same visible issue and might even make things worse.

The Right Molecules for Your Skin Type

For oily skin, salicylic acid is genuinely effective, since it works directly within oil glands to remove excess oil. Tretinoin is another strong option worth considering for oily skin types.

For normal skin with open pores, glycolic acid-based products tend to work best.

For dry, mature skin, the approach shifts toward retinaldehyde or granactive retinoid both part of the retinoid family, but formulated to be gentler than standard retinol while still being effective. Tretinoin can technically work here too, but it needs to be paired with the right moisturizer to avoid making already-dry skin more dehydrated.

Skin Type Recommended Molecule
Oily skin Salicylic acid, Tretinoin
Normal skin Glycolic acid
Dry, mature skin Retinaldehyde, Granactive Retinoid

Busting a Common Myth: Icing Doesn't Shrink Pores

This comes up constantly in consultations, so it's worth addressing directly open pores aren't temperature-sensitive. Icing or steaming your face won't change pore size at all, and extreme temperature exposure can actually damage skin over repeated use. Any improvement you notice from icing is purely temporary, and it's genuinely not worth the downside of repeated extreme temperature exposure on your skin.

The Besan and Multani Mitti Question

Home remedies like besan (gram flour) and multani mitti (Fuller's earth) work well for oily skin with open pores, since both have genuine oil-absorbing properties. But for dry, mature skin, these same remedies do more harm than good they'll absorb the limited natural oil your skin needs, leaving it drier, more dehydrated, and more damaged over time. If your open pores come from dry, mature skin rather than oiliness, skip this remedy entirely.

Building Your Routine: Face Washes

For oily skin, salicylic acid-based face washes work well SPL's face wash comes in both 2% (for very oily skin) and 1% (for moderately oily skin) concentrations. For something slightly more premium, FCL's Alpha Beta Cleanser is a strong option for very oily skin types.

For normal skin with open pores, glycolic acid-based face washes are the better fit. For dry skin, creamy cleansers work best CeraVe's Hydrating Cleanser is a reliable, accessible option, with more premium alternatives also available for those wanting something specifically formulated for dry skin.

Skin Type Face Wash Recommendation
Very oily SPL 2% Salicylic Acid Face Wash
Moderately oily SPL 1% Salicylic Acid Face Wash
Normal Glycolic acid-based face wash
Dry CeraVe Hydrating Cleanser

Moisturizers and Pore Refiners

For oily skin, budget-friendly moisturizer options include formulas like Acne Moist, Acne UV, or Aqualogica moisturizers, with Dermalika's Active Moist being a solid premium pick. For dry skin, richer, more hydrating moisturizers work better, with both budget and premium options available depending on preference.

Pore refiners essentially modified toners containing salicylic acid and niacinamide are worth incorporating too. Unlike traditional toners, which tend to offer limited real benefit, these modified versions actually absorb oil, improve texture, and genuinely help with pore appearance over time.

Serums Worth Trying

Salicylic acid serums work well for oily skin on a budget, with Paula's Choice Salicylic Acid being a solid premium alternative. Glycolic acid comes in both gel and serum formulations, with Bioderma's Glycolic Acid being a reliable premium option.

Tretinoin remains effective for open pores in both oily and dry skin, provided dry skin is paired with the right moisturizer alongside it. That said, tretinoin is prescription-based and shouldn't be self-prescribed it's also unsafe during pregnancy, breastfeeding, or while trying to conceive, so a proper consultation is essential before starting it.

Serum Type Best For
Salicylic acid Oily skin, budget to premium options
Glycolic acid Normal skin, available in gel or serum
Retinaldehyde/Granactive Retinoid Dry, mature skin
Tretinoin Both oily and dry skin (prescription-only)

Sunscreen and Clay Masks

Sunscreen recommendations differ by skin type too for oily skin, lightweight formulas work best, while dry, mature skin benefits from more hydrating sunscreen formulations across budget to premium ranges.

Clay masks, made from bentonite (essentially a version of multani mitti), follow the same rule as the besan and multani mitti remedy great for oily skin with open pores, but genuinely dehydrating and unsuitable for dry, mature skin.

In-Clinic Treatments Worth Considering

For open pores in either oily or dry skin, laser treatments like CO2 laser, Erbium YAG laser, Erbium Glass laser, and MNRF (microneedling radiofrequency) all deliver strong results, though they do come with about a day of downtime.

For those wanting zero downtime, Glass Skin treatment is a favourite it improves texture, corrects open pores, boosts hydration, and supports barrier repair, all without any visible recovery period, meaning you can walk out and go about your day immediately.

Another zero-downtime option worth knowing about is meso-botox, or baby botoxa highly diluted form of botulinum toxin that closes and shrinks open pores with genuinely long-lasting results and no downtime at all.

For dry, mature skin specifically, hydration boosters hyaluronic acid, glutathione, and Vitamin C boosters work particularly well. As hydration improves and collagen remodels, open pores tend to close on their own, alongside genuine improvement in overall skin texture and tone.

Treatment Downtime Best For
CO2/Erbium Laser, MNRF About 1 day Both oily and dry skin, deeper correction
Glass Skin treatment None Texture, hydration, barrier repair
Meso-botox (baby botox) None Pore shrinkage, long-lasting results
Hydration boosters None Dry, mature skin specifically

Getting the Right Approach for Your Skin

This distinction between oily and dry-skin open pores is exactly the kind of nuance that comes up often during consultations at Dadu Medical Centre. Dr. Nivedita Dadu often points out that using the wrong category of product oil-absorbing ingredients on dry, mature skin, for instance can actually worsen the very concern you're trying to fix. If you're unsure which category your open pores fall into, consulting a dermatologist in Delhi helps avoid wasted time and money on mismatched products.

For anyone exploring a proper skin clinic in Delhi offering treatments like Glass Skin, meso-botox, or hydration boosters alongside traditional laser options, understanding your skin type first makes that consultation far more productive. A good clinic in Delhi will assess whether your open pores stem from oiliness or reduced elasticity before recommending a specific product or procedure.

Final Thoughts

Open pores aren't a single, universal problem the cause differs entirely between oily and dry, mature skin, and so does the solution. What works beautifully for oily skin can genuinely worsen dry skin, and vice versa. Getting your skin type and underlying cause right is the real starting point, whether you're building an at-home routine or considering an in-clinic treatment.
