How Diode Laser Works

The laser gives off infrared light at roughly 800 to 810nm. Dark hair contains melanin, and melanin soaks up this light. The energy travels down the hair shaft into the follicle, heats it, and leaves it far less able to produce new hair.

Why does that matter for Indian skin? A longer wavelength like this goes deeper and puts less of its energy into the pigment near the skin's surface, which lowers the risk for medium to dark complexions. The handpiece has cooling built in as well, so the skin gets chilled every time a pulse goes out.

What the laser can't do is treat every hair at once. Only follicles that are actively growing respond, and at any given moment plenty of them are resting. Spacing out the sessions lets us catch them as they wake up.

Diode vs Other Options

Shaving, threading and waxing are fast and familiar. They're also short-lived, and years of waxing can leave ingrown hairs and uneven texture behind. Laser works on a different idea altogether: long-term reduction instead of starting over every few weeks.

Among lasers, diode and Alexandrite (755nm) are the two people ask about most. Alexandrite sits shallower and tends to suit finer, lighter hair on fairer skin. Diode is usually the better match for coarse, dark hair. And if you tried laser years ago and didn't enjoy it, you're not imagining things. The older machines really were slower and more painful, and mostly only worked on dark hair over pale skin. Current diode systems are far kinder.

FAQs

Why does diode suit thick, dark hair?

Dark, coarse hair carries more pigment for the laser to latch onto, and the wavelength reaches deep enough to affect the root.

Is it safe on Indian skin?

Generally, yes. The long wavelength puts less energy into surface pigment, so it suits medium and darker skin better than shorter lasers do.

Will it hurt?

It's usually comfortable. The tip is cooled, so your skin stays chilled while the pulses go in.

How many sessions should I plan for?

Six to eight. Depending on the area, they're spaced four to eight weeks apart.

Why can't it be done in one go?

Only actively growing follicles respond, and they don't all grow at the same time. Each session catches a fresh set.

Do I need time off afterwards?

No. You can head back to work or your plans right away.

Does the hair come back?

What returns is finer, lighter and much sparser. A small amount may reappear over the years, and a top-up session deals with it.

Can I get my face done?

Yes. The upper lip and chin are common requests, and facial sessions are usually four to five weeks apart.
