Scabies in Winter: Why It Keeps Coming Back and How to Actually Get Rid of It

Every year, right around the time the weather turns cold or the monsoon humidity sets in, clinics across the city start seeing the same pattern: a sudden spike in patients complaining of relentless itching, especially at night. If this sounds familiar, you're not imagining things, and you're definitely not alone. This is actually one of the most searched concerns we get for treatment in Delhi during the winter months, and there's a very specific reason for it.

Why Does Scabies Show Up More in Winter?

Scabies is caused by a tiny mite called Sarcoptes scabiei, and this little organism genuinely loves humidity and cooler temperatures. Rainy season, winter, these are its favourite conditions to multiply. On the flip side, when temperatures shoot up in peak summer, the mite simply can't survive as well. So if you've noticed that your itching problem seems to disappear every summer only to return like clockwork each winter, that's not a coincidence at all, that's just the mite's biology at work.

Is It Safe to Treat Scabies During Pregnancy?

This comes up a lot, and the answer is genuinely reassuring yes, it's completely safe. Permethrin cream at 5% concentration is considered safe to use even during pregnancy. The same goes for babies too, though there's an age cutoff: permethrin cream can be used in babies above two months of age.

What About Kids and Special Areas Like the Face?

Here's something a lot of adults get confused about. In grown-ups, scabies typically stay below the neck; it almost never affects the face. But for children, it's a different story. Scabies can absolutely show up on a child's face, and it can also involve the palms and soles, which is unusual for adults. So when treating children, the permethrin lotion needs to be applied on the face, palms, and soles as well, not just the rest of the body.

"I've Had It for Five Years and Nothing's Working" Sound Familiar?

If you've been dealing with scabies on and off for years despite applying cream repeatedly, there's usually a missing piece in the puzzle. Scabies mites don't just live on your skin they hide in the lining of your clothes, bedsheets, quilts, and mattresses too. Simply washing your clothes normally, or even with Dettol, won't kill them. The mites only die with high heat.

Step What to Do
1 Boil water and dip clothes/bedsheets into it
2 Let the water cool down completely before removing the clothes
3 Wash the clothes normally after they've cooled
4 For items that can't be boiled, sun-dry them or iron them thoroughly
5 Repeat for all clothes worn recently including underwear

Skipping this step is honestly the biggest reason people end up stuck in a cycle of scabies coming back every single year around the same season.

Why Does Scabies Keep Recurring for Some People?

If you've done everything right and it's still coming back, incomplete treatment is usually the first culprit. But there are a few other reasons worth considering too. People with diabetes tend to pick up scabies more easily, and it's often harder to clear them. Similarly, anyone who's immunocompromised whether due to HIV, cancer treatment, or another condition may find that their weakened immune defences make the infection stubborn to shift.

For people in this category, permethrin alone might not cut it. There are other molecules worth discussing with your doctor, including crotamiton, or combination creams with cetrimide and permethrin.

Quick Reference: Common Scabies Questions Answered

Any food restrictions during scabies? None this is a mite infestation, not diet-related

"But I'm Very Hygienic. How Did I Even Get This?"

This is probably the question we hear the most, and honestly, the assumption behind it is where most people go wrong. Scabies has nothing to do with how clean you are. It spreads through close contact and shared spaces, hostels, hospitals, hotels, even prisons, anywhere multiple people share bedding or living spaces. If someone in your household, or your domestic help, or even someone you stayed with briefly has scabies, it can pass on to you regardless of how hygienic you personally are.

Is Night-Time Itching Always Scabies?

Not necessarily. It's true that scabies mites become more active at night, which is exactly why permethrin is applied at bedtime; it gets absorbed properly and targets the mites when they surface. But itching that worsens at night isn't unique to scabies. It's actually common with most skin conditions simply because we're less distracted in the evening and more likely to notice and scratch.

Pregnancy is another situation where itching doesn't always mean scabies. There are several pregnancy-specific itching conditions that have nothing to do with mites at all, so if you're pregnant and itchy, it's worth getting a proper evaluation rather than assuming the worst. Your gynaecologist can always refer you to a dermatologist in Delhi if something seems out of the ordinary.

One Last Thing Diet

People often ask if there's anything they need to avoid eating during scabies treatment. There isn't. This is purely a mite infestation, and there's no dietary restriction involved the focus needs to be entirely on proper topical treatment and decontaminating your clothes and bedding.

At Dadu Medical Centre, we see this pattern repeat every season, and the good news is that scabies is almost always completely treatable when the full protocol medication plus proper clothing and bedding decontamination is followed correctly. If you're dealing with recurring itching that just won't quit, it's worth consulting a skin specialist in Delhi rather than continuing to self-treat with over-the-counter creams alone.
